\paragraph{Probabilities and prior shift (Member 5).}
Accuracy only checks which side of $0.5$ a probability falls on, but a cost-sensitive filter acts on the
value of the probability. On Spambase, scaling the logistic model's log-odds left its accuracy unchanged at
$93.6\%$ yet raised its cost by a third, and only log-loss and the reliability diagram exposed the change.
Calibration should therefore be checked before deploying a model, and Platt scaling on validation data is
a cheap repair. When the spam rate changes but spam looks the same, one constant shift of the log-odds
restores the Bayes rule. For logistic regression this is an intercept change, and for the Gaussian model it
is a change of priors. The shift is only as good as the calibration underneath it.
